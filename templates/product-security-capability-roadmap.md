Build capabilities according to product exposure, engineering demand and business criticality. Stages describe operating needs, **not** company size, mandatory job titles or a prescribed headcount.

## Stage A: Establish ownership and trust

**Possible triggers:** unclear application owners, late security involvement, consequential integrations without requirements or untracked exceptions.

| Capability | Minimum useful practice | Who could perform it | Evidence of progress | Resource dependency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Business-aware Product Security lead | Partner on highest-consequence design decisions | Existing security leader or designated specialist | Named review and consultation path | |
| Product and engineering ownership | Identify accountable owners and change paths | Existing Engineering / Product leads | Confirmed inventory for critical workflows | |
| Risk decisions | Define delegation, escalation, register and expiration | Leadership with Security GRC support | Authorized policy and tracked exceptions | |
| Secure development support | Provide a few maintained patterns and practical guidance | Product Security and Engineering | Adoption in material workflows | |
| Independent assurance | Obtain targeted external expertise where justified | Authorized specialist | Scoped findings and follow-up | |

## Stage B: Embed secure engineering

**Possible triggers:** repeated similar defects, growing integrations, more releases, slow security consultations or material remediation backlog.

| Capability | Improvement | Potential capacity response | Evidence / review point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earlier design partnership | Security reviews consequential design changes when decisions can still change | Dedicated Product Security engineer or allocated specialist time | Engagement at design stage |
| Engineering champions | Train and allocate time for local advocates | Champions with explicit time and manager support | Reused patterns and solved issues |
| Shared security components | Assigned maintainers, versioning and documentation | Engineering / platform capacity plus specialist review | Support and adoption records |
| Findings ownership | Remediation scheduling, validation and recurrence learning | Existing engineering owners and risk coordination | Verified closures and repeat patterns |

## Stage C: Scale specialized assurance and response

**Possible triggers:** multiple product lines, sensitive or privileged agent actions, high-volume third-party integrations, recurring incidents or sustained specialist review demand.

| Capability | Potential investment | Build / partner / buy question | Evidence that capacity is justified |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specialist product security engineering | Dedicated threat modeling, auth, cloud/product review or testing expertise | Consistent demand versus external bursts | Review queue, exposure and complexity |
| Security automation and research | Controlled automated review, approved agent workflows and evaluated tooling | Does it improve decision quality and timely remediation? | Measured false positives and verified actions |
| Product telemetry and response | Defined handoffs with Security Platform | Shared internal operation or specialist partnership | Incident response lessons and coverage |
| GRC integration | Durable exception, obligation and evidence systems | Internal workflow engineering or supported service | Timely reviews and auditability |

## Culture and outcome measures

Measure whether engineers voluntarily involve Security early, security guidance is usable, critical systems have owners, repeat defects decline and accepted risks get treated on schedule. Capture baseline and measurement method before setting goals. Avoid using scan count, meeting attendance or raw finding counts as evidence of reduced risk.
